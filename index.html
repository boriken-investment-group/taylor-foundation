Why Taylor foundations move

Your house sits on clay that moves with the seasons

Taylor is in eastern Williamson County, where the soils are dark blackland clays rather than the hillier, limey soils of the county's western half. Blackland Prairie clays like Houston Black, the official state soil of Texas, swell as they take on water and shrink as they dry out. A foundation on top of that soil gets pushed in wet seasons and loses support in dry ones. Over the years, that uneven movement shows up as cracks, sticking doors and sloping floors.

What to look for

Signs worth getting checked

Doors and windows that stick

Especially ones that used to close fine, or that only stick in certain seasons.

Diagonal cracks at corners

Cracks running up and away from the corners of door frames and windows.

Stair-step cracks in brick

Cracks that follow the mortar lines in a step pattern on the outside walls.

Floors that slope or bounce

A marble that rolls on its own, or a floor that feels soft or uneven underfoot.

Gaps that open up

Between walls and ceilings, around window frames, or where trim pulls away.

Cracks that keep widening

In slab floors, tile, or the visible edge of the foundation outside.

A single hairline crack on its own is common and often cosmetic. What matters is change: cracks that widen, doors that start sticking, gaps that grow. Take dated photos so whoever assesses your home can see how things have moved.

Taylor rules

Taylor requires a permit for foundation repair

The City of Taylor publishes a specific checklist for foundation repair permits, applied for through its online portal. What it asks for depends on your foundation:

  • Slab repair: a stamped and signed engineer's plan of the whole foundation, showing the area to be repaired
  • Pier and beam shimming: a layout plan showing every pier, the spacing, and which piers need shims
  • Pier and beam repair: the pier layout plus an engineer-signed and sealed section drawing
  • Every job: a written scope of work describing the repair method and how much is being repaired

A reputable company handles the permit for you. If anyone suggests skipping it, treat that as a warning sign. Read the full Taylor permit guide.

Protect yourself

Before you sign anything

Texas doesn't license foundation repair contractors, so there's no state license to look up. That puts more of the homework on you.

  1. Consider your own engineer. An independent structural engineer, paid by you rather than the repair company, decides how many piers you need and where. That decision sets the price. One consumer guide puts the cost at a few hundred dollars to around $1,500.
  2. Compare bids on the same scope. Ask for written quotes based on the same engineer's plan, so you're comparing the same job rather than different guesses.
  3. Read the warranty itself. Ask for the warranty document, not just the promise, and check whether it transfers if you sell the house.
  4. Confirm the permit. In Taylor a slab repair can't be permitted without an engineer's stamped plan. Make sure it's being pulled.

Source for engineer cost and bid advice: Foundation Select, red flags guide.

Questions

Straight answers

Does Texas license foundation repair contractors?
No. Texas does not issue a state license for foundation repair, and there is no statewide general contractor license either. Cities set their own rules. In Taylor that means a city permit, and for slab repairs an engineer's stamped plan.
Do I need a permit for foundation repair in Taylor?
Yes. The City of Taylor publishes a foundation repair permit checklist, applied for through its online permit portal. A slab repair needs a stamped and signed engineer's plan. A reputable repair company normally handles the permit as part of the job.
